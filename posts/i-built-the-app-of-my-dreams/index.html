I built the app of my dreams

As I get older, I visit doctors and clinics more often. Every visit leaves me with another piece of paper or another prescription, and none of them are connected.

In the room I never have the whole picture. What changed since last time? Which medicine did I start, and when? What did I want to ask? I remember half of it on the train home.

I wanted one place that answers those questions before I walk in. So I built Better Me.

The papers

Most of my health history is on paper, in Japanese, in a drawer. A pharmacy slip, a lab report, a note from a clinic. If the app only worked with data that is already digital, it would miss most of what matters.

So the papers go in. I keep the original next to the values I pulled from it, because I don’t trust a number I can’t trace back to the page. Anything the app is unsure about, I check myself. Anything it can’t read stays empty. I would rather have a gap than a wrong value in front of my doctor.

The app doesn’t tell me a result is good or bad. That is my doctor’s job. I only want to arrive with the numbers in order.

Mine

These records are about my body, so they stay on my iPhone. No account, no cloud, no AI service reading my lab results. I decide what leaves the phone, and for each report I choose the dates and sections my doctor sees.

It works in English and Japanese, because my life does.

I’m not a doctor and this is not medical advice. I still take the results to mine.
