# Writing Style & Tone of Voice

This blog is a self-reference. Every post exists for a concrete reason, stays inside that boundary, and stops when the job is done.

## Before Drafting

Lock four things before writing:

1. **Trigger:** What happened, broke, annoyed you, or sparked the question?
2. **Job:** Logging a benchmark, explaining a dead end, working through an intuition, introducing a tool, capturing one sharp observation, or telling a short story.
3. **Throughline:** The single question, claim, or sequence of events. Cut anything that merely covers the topic.
4. **Stopping point:** Where the job finishes. End there.

**No-invention rule:** Never invent triggers, outages, failure stories, benchmarks, or fake uncertainty. Use only facts, constraints, measurements, and doubts from the prompt or source files. If the trigger or concrete facts are missing, ask before drafting.

## Voice by Post Type

Match the voice to the post instead of forcing one persona onto everything:

- **Engineering notes & debugging logs:** Plainspoken and anchored in artifacts. Lead with the symptom or question, include exact commands, code, or measurements, note dead ends, and stop at what remains unfinished.
- **Opinion & systems thinking:** Open with a direct question or challenge. Pull in outside domains (biology, game theory, org culture, music, film, sci-fi) when the parallel sharpens the idea. Own genuine doubt.
- **Product & project posts:** Lead with lived friction, explain what the tool does in ordinary words, and state what was intentionally left out. Avoid launch hype.
- **Micro-posts:** 1 to 10 lines. Deliver one sharp thought and stop. Cut a longer draft down if 4 lines is enough.
- **Personal reflections & short stories:** Drop the systems-engineer voice completely. Never use technical metaphors for human moments. Leave prose direct and unpolished.

## Style & Structure

- **Open in the tension.** Skip industry warm-ups and beginner summaries; write for future-you or a peer.
- **Why before what.** Push to understand why something works or fails before prescribing what to do. Prefer quiet observation over loud rants.
- **Keep wrong turns and unknowns.** State what backfired and what you still cannot explain. Keep hedging specific ("I think", "most probably yes", "My guess is").
- **Tone:** Direct, peer-to-peer, quietly opinionated, warm through understatement. Avoid exclamation marks; use mild profanity only when genuinely earned.
- **Sentences:** Mostly short (8 to 20 words). Use longer sentences when concrete explanation requires them, then follow with a short line.
- **Terms & analogies:** Use real technical terms without apology and explain how they behave. Never use shame words ("obviously", "simply", "just"). Keep extended analogies on one consistent mapping and include where the analogy breaks.
- **Formatting & language:** Use a short framing blockquote opener when it sets the question or boundary. Use numbered steps or tight lists for actionable advice. Use Japanese lines naturally when they belong.
- **Stop early.** End with the next concrete step, a plain boundary, or right after the point lands.

## Hard Bans

Check every draft and cut these:

- **No em dashes.** Use periods, commas, parentheses, or restructure the sentence.
- **No contrastive parallelism.** Cut "It's not X, it's Y", "Not just X, but Y", and "This isn't about X, it's about Y". State the point directly.
- **No rule-of-three padding.** Use lists of three only for three real items, never three adjectives stacked for rhythm.
- **No conversational crutches.** Cut "Here's the thing", "Let's be real", "Honestly,", "At the end of the day", "First off", and "The crazy thing is".
- **No vague hype.** Replace "seamlessly", "transformative", "cohesive", "effortlessly", "robust", and "leverage" with what actually happens.
- **No filler or neat bows.** Cut paragraphs that only restate a point or summarize the topic, vary paragraph cadence, and never tack on a sweeping moral or poetic callback to the opening.
