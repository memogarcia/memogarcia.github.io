    Hour by hour, 0 to 72

    The glass holds the whole fast. It is full when you start and empty at your goal, so the waterline sits at the hour you are in. The etched lines are where the body changes fuel: digestion, glycogen, ketosis, autophagy and the deep end of an extended fast. Presets cover 12:12, 14:10, 16:8, 18:6, 20:4, OMAD (24h), 36h, 48h and 72h, or any goal up to a week.

    0 to 3 hours Digestion

    1. 0h

      Meal digestion begins

      Food enters the stomach and upper intestine. Blood glucose begins rising as carbohydrates start breaking down.

      Fuel: Dietary carbs & fat · Insulin: Rising · Nutrient uptake

    2. 1h

      Glucose absorption peaks

      Pancreatic insulin rises to move absorbed glucose into muscle, liver, and fat cells while suppressing fat release.

      Fuel: Dietary glucose · Insulin: Peaking · Energy storage

    3. 2h

      Glycogen storage and protein uptake

      Muscle and liver top off glycogen reserves and absorb amino acids from your last meal.

      Fuel: Dietary glucose · Insulin: High, tapering · Glycogen synthesis

    4. 3h

      Gastric emptying slows down

      Most simple carbohydrates have cleared the stomach, and insulin begins stepping down from its post-meal peak.

      Fuel: Late dietary nutrients · Insulin: Descending · Post-meal taper

    4 to 11 hours Glycogen drawdown

    1. 4h

      Post-absorptive transition

      Gut nutrient delivery fades and the liver starts releasing stored glycogen to keep blood sugar steady.

      Fuel: Blood glucose + liver glycogen · Insulin: Falling · Glucagon rises

    2. 5h

      Insulin settles toward baseline

      Lower insulin eases the brake on stored triglycerides, allowing early fat mobilization to warm up.

      Fuel: Liver glycogen · Insulin: Trending low · Storage switch closes

    3. 6h

      Gut motility sweeps clean

      Housekeeping waves sweep residual food and bacteria down the small intestine, giving the upper gut a quiet break.

      Fuel: Liver glycogen · Insulin: Low · Migrating motor complex

    4. 7h

      Digestive workload drops

      Less blood flow and metabolic effort go toward digestion, which often reduces post-meal sluggishness.

      Fuel: Liver glycogen + fatty acids · Insulin: Low · Resting gut

    5. 8h

      Lipolysis begins contributing

      Adipose tissue releases more free fatty acids into circulation so skeletal muscle can spare remaining glucose.

      Fuel: Glycogen + free fatty acids · Insulin: Low · Fat release starts

    6. 9h

      Peripheral tissues shift to fat

      Resting muscles lean more on fatty acids for fuel, preserving liver glycogen for the brain and red blood cells.

      Fuel: Glycogen + fatty acids · Insulin: Low · Glucose sparing

    7. 10h

      Gluconeogenesis supports glucose

      As liver glycogen thins out, the liver steps up gluconeogenesis to keep blood glucose stable without food.

      Fuel: Glycogen + glycerol/amino acids · Insulin: Low baseline · Endogenous glucose

    8. 11h

      Fat mobilization accelerates

      Insulin rests near fasting baseline while fatty acid oxidation climbs toward the metabolic switch point.

      Fuel: Fatty acids + waning glycogen · Insulin: Low baseline · Pre-ketosis ramp

    12 to 23 hours Fat and ketone shift

    1. 12h

      Metabolic switch threshold

      For many adults, liver glycogen reaches low levels here and fat breakdown becomes the primary source of resting fuel.

      Fuel: Free fatty acids + early ketones · Insulin: Fasting baseline · Glycogen low

    2. 13h

      Early ketone production starts

      The liver converts surplus acetyl-CoA from fat oxidation into beta-hydroxybutyrate (BHB) for brain and heart fuel.

      Fuel: Fatty acids + BHB · Insulin: Fasting baseline · Hepatic ketogenesis

    3. 14h

      Fat oxidation climbs, hunger pulses pass

      Fat burning deepens. Hunger waves triggered by habit often peak for 15 to 20 minutes and fade when hydrated.

      Fuel: Fatty acids + rising ketones · Insulin: Very low · Ghrelin wave

    4. 15h

      Measurable ketone lift for most fasts

      Blood ketones rise above fed baseline, providing steady backup energy with fewer blood sugar swings.

      Fuel: Fat + ketones · Insulin: Very low · Cleaner fuel mix

    5. 16h

      16:8 daily intermittent threshold

      Classic 16:8 target. Low insulin and steady fat oxidation support metabolic flexibility and calm focus.

      Fuel: Predominantly fat + ketones · Insulin: Very low · Steady lipolysis

    6. 17h

      Mild norepinephrine preserves alertness

      Gentle sympathetic tone helps maintain resting energy expenditure and mental sharpness despite no food intake.

      Fuel: Fat + ketones · Insulin: Very low · Alertness preserved

    7. 18h

      Cellular cleanup signals rise

      Low energy availability activates AMPK and quiets mTOR, priming early cellular recycling alongside deeper fat burn.

      Fuel: Fat + ketones · Insulin: Very low · AMPK rises, mTOR drops

    8. 19h

      Growth hormone pulses preserve lean mass

      Higher growth hormone pulse amplitude supports fat mobilization while helping protect skeletal muscle protein.

      Fuel: Fat + ketones · Insulin: Very low · HGH pulsatility

    9. 20h

      20:4 milestone: deep fat reliance

      Liver glycogen is largely spent, so mobilized body fat and ketones cover almost all resting caloric demand.

      Fuel: Fatty acids + ketones · Insulin: Very low · Liver glycogen minimal

    10. 21h

      Brain draws a larger share from ketones

      Higher BHB availability reduces the brain's glucose requirement, easing pressure on amino acid breakdown.

      Fuel: Fatty acids + ketones · Insulin: Very low · Neural ketone use

    11. 22h

      Inflammatory signaling dips

      With zero postprandial glucose spikes for nearly a day, circulating inflammatory cytokines tend to settle.

      Fuel: Fatty acids + ketones · Insulin: Very low · Lower post-meal stress

    12. 23h

      Approaching the 24-hour mark

      Ketogenesis and cellular housekeeping build momentum as the digestive tract enjoys nearly a full day of rest.

      Fuel: Fatty acids + ketones · Insulin: Very low · Repair transition

    24 to 47 hours Autophagy and repair

    1. 24h

      24-hour (OMAD) milestone

      Cellular recycling (autophagy) ramps up to break down worn organelles and misfolded proteins while ketones climb.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Autophagy active

    2. 25h

      Extended gut rest and mucosal turnover

      A full day without digestive load reduces gut barrier friction and supports epithelial recovery.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Intestinal rest

    3. 26h

      BHB acts as a signaling molecule

      Beyond fuel, beta-hydroxybutyrate signals antioxidant and stress-resistance gene pathways.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Oxidative defense

    4. 27h

      Elevated growth hormone supports lean tissue

      Sustained growth hormone release shifts substrate preference away from protein breakdown toward adipose stores.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Protein sparing

    5. 28h

      Intracellular lipid clearance deepens

      Liver and muscle cells clear stored ectopic fat droplets, supporting stronger insulin sensitivity when you next eat.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Insulin sensitivity

    6. 29h

      Day-one hunger often softens

      Steady ketones and flatter blood glucose frequently blunt acute hunger signals compared with hours 12 to 20.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Appetite steadies

    7. 30h

      Mitochondrial quality control

      Damaged mitochondria are tagged and recycled (mitophagy), favoring more efficient energy-producing networks.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Mitophagy active

    8. 31h

      Watch water and sodium balance

      Low insulin increases kidney sodium excretion; plain water plus electrolytes helps prevent fatigue or headaches.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Natriuresis check

    9. 32h

      Overwhelmingly fat-derived expenditure

      Lipolysis and ketogenesis supply the vast majority of resting energy needs while blood sugar stays tight.

      Fuel: Fatty acids + ketones · Insulin: Minimal · Deep fat oxidation

    10. 33h

      BDNF neuro-support pathways active

      Ketones and mild metabolic stress promote brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), tied to synaptic resilience.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · BDNF signaling

    11. 34h

      Overnight deep metabolic adaptation

      Maintaining sleep and hydration through day two keeps cortisol steadier and supports cellular maintenance.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Calm autonomic tone

    12. 35h

      Nutritional ketosis deepens

      Many adults cross higher ketone concentrations here (often around 1.0 mmol/L or above, varying by diet and activity).

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Higher BHB range

    13. 36h

      36-hour milestone: sustained cellular renewal

      Continuous low insulin and strong AMPK signaling keep autophagy and fat oxidation running smoothly.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Full-day fast complete

    14. 37h

      Protein conservation tightens

      As brain tissues adapt further to ketone bodies, daily protein catabolism drops below day-one rates.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Nitrogen sparing

    15. 38h

      Adipose release stays steady

      Blood glucose remains remarkably flat without post-meal swings while fat stores fuel daily activity.

      Fuel: Fatty acids + ketones · Insulin: Minimal · Flat glycemic curve

    16. 39h

      Antioxidant defenses stay upregulated

      Stress-response proteins help protect cells against reactive oxygen species during fat-heavy metabolism.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Cellular resilience

    17. 40h

      Hepatic fat burden eases

      Extended fasting gives the liver space to clear stored triglycerides and restore tighter metabolic signaling.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Liver clearance

    18. 41h

      Tissue-wide cleanup continues

      Autophagy across liver, muscle, and neural tissues continues clearing aging protein aggregates.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Lysosomal turnover

    19. 42h

      Mental clarity when electrolytes hold

      Many report calm mental steadiness at this stage, provided sodium, potassium, and fluids are kept balanced.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Ketone-fueled focus

    20. 43h

      Glycerol sustains essential glucose

      Fat breakdown supplies glycerol backbones to cover essential glucose without leaning heavily on muscle tissue.

      Fuel: Ketones + fat + glycerol · Insulin: Minimal · Minimal muscle draw

    21. 44h

      Digestive enzyme demand stays quiet

      Digestive secretions remain minimal, letting the gut lining stay calm prior to eventual refeeding.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Deep GI rest

    22. 45h

      Growth hormone pulses stay elevated

      Counter-regulatory hormones help preserve lean structure as you approach two full days.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Tissue defense

    23. 46h

      Leukocyte energy conservation shifts

      Lower IGF-1 and PKA signaling begin nudging white blood cell turnover toward conservation and renewal.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Immune signaling

    24. 47h

      Approaching the 48-hour threshold

      Check hydration, sodium, and how you feel physically before continuing into a third fasting day.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Electrolyte check

    48 to 72 hours Extended fast

    1. 48h

      48-hour milestone: low IGF-1 and deep repair

      Two days in, baseline insulin and IGF-1 drop substantially, keeping cellular maintenance prioritized over growth.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · IGF-1 reduced

    2. 49h

      Repair mode holds over anabolic growth

      Low IGF-1 signaling reduces proliferative pressure and favors cellular stress-resistance programs.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Low mTOR/IGF-1

    3. 50h

      Brain ketone share rises further

      Neural tissues cover a large majority of energy demand with ketones, conserving remaining protein reserves.

      Fuel: High ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Deep keto-adaptation

    4. 51h

      Inflammatory tone remains quiet

      Absence of dietary inflammatory triggers and steady BHB signaling help keep baseline inflammation low.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Low oxidative load

    5. 52h

      Keep exertion gentle

      Light walking and mobility feel good here, while high-intensity training is better saved until after refeeding.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Gentle movement

    6. 53h

      Prolonged immune signaling adjustments

      Extended nutrient scarcity continues signaling older immune cells to clear out in readiness for renewal.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · White cell turnover

    7. 54h

      Listen closely to physical cues

      At 54 hours, persistent dizziness, palpitations, or weakness are clear signals to stop and refeed slowly.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Safety check-in

    8. 55h

      Steady hepatic ketogenesis

      The liver cleanly converts mobilized adipose fatty acids into circulating ketones at a stable multi-day pace.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Liver fat export

    9. 56h

      Ketones settle toward multi-day plateau

      In clinical fasting studies, blood ketones often climb toward 2 to 4 mmol/L around day two to three.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Ketone plateau

    10. 57h

      Sustained intracellular recycling

      Lysosomal turnover continues clearing damaged cellular debris while nutrient-sensing pathways stay quiet.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Deep autophagy

    11. 58h

      Intestinal stem cell readiness

      Animal and organoid fasting research points to heightened intestinal stem cell readiness for post-fast renewal.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Gut priming

    12. 59h

      Stand up slowly and stay hydrated

      Lower blood volume from water and sodium loss makes slow posture changes and electrolytes important.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Blood pressure care

    13. 60h

      60-hour mark: fat-adapted fasting state

      Two and a half days without calories leaves exogenous glucose long gone and lipid metabolism fully dominant.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Full lipid reliance

    14. 61h

      Cellular defense programs remain active

      Continued low insulin and IGF-1 keep maintenance and antioxidant genes active without dietary interruption.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Stress-resistance

    15. 62h

      High protein conservation efficiency

      Endogenous glucose demand stays low enough that lean tissue breakdown remains tightly conserved.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Muscle sparing

    16. 63h

      Steady neural ketone oxidation

      Efficient brain uptake of acetoacetate and BHB supports alertness late into day three.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Cognitive steadiness

    17. 64h

      Prolonged oxidative and inflammatory calm

      Systemic inflammatory markers often bottom out relative to baseline when hydration and sleep remain solid.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Systemic quiet

    18. 65h

      Plan a gentle refeeding meal

      Prepare light broth, cooked vegetables, or small protein portions rather than a large sugar- or fat-heavy meal.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Refeed planning

    19. 66h

      Digestive enzymes sit downregulated

      Because stomach acid and digestive enzymes have scaled down, breaking the fast slowly prevents cramping.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Protect gut comfort

    20. 67h

      Final stretch of a 3-day fast

      Mild chilliness is common as energy conserves; dress warmly and avoid strenuous output.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Warmth & rest

    21. 68h

      Deep lipid mobilization continues

      Fatty acid release and ketone synthesis stay steady while insulin sits near its physiological floor.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Sustained ketosis

    22. 69h

      Immune renewal signaling deepens

      Prolonged low PKA and IGF-1 signaling primes hematopoietic stem cell renewal upon careful refeeding.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Hematopoietic cue

    23. 70h

      Two hours from the 72-hour mark

      Have broth or a gentle first bite ready so ending the fast stays unhurried and controlled.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Transition readiness

    24. 71h

      Final hour toward 72 hours

      Cellular recycling, ketone fuel use, and immune reset signaling sit at multi-day depth.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Peak multi-day state

    25. 72h

      72-hour milestone: prolonged fast complete

      Deep ketosis, sustained autophagy, and immune renewal signaling. Refeed slowly and consult a doctor before going past 72 hours.

      Fuel: Ketones + fatty acids · Insulin: Minimal · Stem cell & autophagy cue
